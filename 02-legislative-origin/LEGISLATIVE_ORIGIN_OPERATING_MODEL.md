# Legislative Origin Operating Model

Congress is the first legislative-origin module in this repository, not the sole source of governmental action. The model records a measure's observable path without treating sponsorship, committee activity, voting, or enactment as interchangeable events.

## Recordable chain

`measure identity -> sponsor/introduction -> referral -> committee action -> floor action -> cross-chamber disposition -> presentment -> executive disposition -> public-law or other final status -> operative provision link`

| Stage | Required proof question |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | What exact measure, Congress, version, and official identifier are being discussed? |
| Introduction | Who introduced it, when, and under what official record? |
| Committee | Which committee or subcommittee received, changed, reported, or declined it? |
| Floor action | What text/version was considered and what action occurred? |
| Cross-chamber disposition | Did the other chamber take corresponding action on an identifiable version? |
| Presentment/disposition | Was there presentment and what executive disposition is recorded? |

## Non-equivalences

- Sponsorship is not enactment.
- A hearing is not a rule.
- Committee activity is not final passage.
- Passage of one chamber is not bicameral enactment.
- Enactment is not an unlimited delegation.
- A statute is not proof that a later application is current, in scope, procedurally valid, or evidentially supported.

This batch supplies the record contract and operating model. It does not ingest bills, votes, members, or legal conclusions.
