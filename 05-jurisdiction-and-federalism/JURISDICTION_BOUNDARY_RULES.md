# Jurisdiction and Federalism Boundary Rules

Jurisdiction is evaluated for the particular asserted action, not assumed from the existence of an institution. A record must identify the actor, claimed source or authority basis, subject scope, geographic scope, institutional boundary, time boundary where material, and conflict rule where one is asserted.

Federal, state, territorial, tribal, local, judicial, executive, legislative, and private-actor boundaries remain explicit. Shared or overlapping responsibility does not erase the need to identify the particular authority and limit at issue.

This repository records boundary claims and their evidence paths; it does not conclusively resolve constitutional disputes.
