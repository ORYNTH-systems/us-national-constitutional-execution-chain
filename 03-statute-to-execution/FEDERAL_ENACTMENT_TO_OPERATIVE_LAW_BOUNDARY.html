Federal Enactment to Operative Law Boundary

Enactment is not automatic execution. A measure may be enacted while a particular provision is not yet effective, is condition-dependent, is unfunded, has expired, has been amended or repealed, is limited by judicial effect, or does not authorize the proposed actor or consequence.

Required post-enactment resolution

  1. Bind the enactment route to the authenticated enrolled text and law identity.
  2. Decompose the law into operative provisions without altering the source text.
  3. Resolve definitions, scope, regulated or benefited objects, exceptions, effective dates, triggers, sunset, savings clauses, and dependencies.
  4. Determine current temporal status using preserved amendment, correction, repeal, expiration, supersession, and judicial-effect records.
  5. Identify the office, jurisdiction, delegation, procedure, appropriation, evidence standard, consequence gate, receipt, review, and remedy required for implementation.
  6. Stop if any required element is absent, conflicted, stale, exceeded, or unresolved.

Non-equivalences

  • Bill is not law.
  • One-chamber passage is not bicameral passage.
  • Bicameral passage is not enactment before the applicable presentment route completes.
  • Enactment is not publication.
  • Publication is not codification.
  • Codification is not the exclusive source-law record.
  • Enacted provision is not necessarily currently operative.
  • Operative provision is not self-executing authority for every office.
  • Delegated implementation is not unlimited discretion.
  • Administrative action is not valid merely because an agency performed it.
  • Automated capability is never public authority by itself.
