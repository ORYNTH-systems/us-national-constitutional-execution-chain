# About

## Ashley S. Harris and ORYNTH Systems

The National Constitutional Execution Reference Framework was created by **Ashley S. Harris**, an independent systems researcher and founder and principal of **ORYNTH Systems**.

Her work examines execution governance: the conditions under which authority, evidence, decision, and consequential effect must remain distinct, attributable, and reviewable in human and automated systems.

## Why this framework exists

This work began with a narrow question: how can a public claim of authority be examined without treating an institutional label, a web page, an available capability, or a successful action as proof that the authority exists or was lawfully exercised?

The answer required tracing the chain downstream. A constitutional source may relate to offices, jurisdiction, delegation, procedure, evidence, admissibility, execution, effect, provenance, challenge, and remedy. A break at any material point must remain visible rather than be concealed by an asserted outcome.

This repository records that architecture as a public, reconstructible reference framework. It is not a governmental system, does not represent any government institution, and does not replace constitutional interpretation, lawful public office, judicial review, or public participation.

## Methodological posture

ORYNTH's work is oriented toward systems that can be inspected before they are trusted. In this framework, source is not interpretation; evidence is not authority; capability is not permission; a proposal is not an admission; and a decision is not a consequential governmental effect.

The framework deliberately preserves unresolved obligations and blocks consequential transitions until the specified evidence and accountable human decision conditions are satisfied. Its purpose is to make both support and uncertainty legible.
