National Constitutional Execution Reference Framework

A fail-closed reference architecture for tracing how public authority may move through a constitutional system.

This framework begins from a simple discipline: authority must remain attributable, bounded, admissible, and verifiable. It separates source from interpretation, evidence from authority, capability from permission, proposal from admission, and finality from loss of provenance.

Six connected reference slices

  1. Congressional execution: institutional roles, rosters, term context, and legislative evidence are routed for review rather than treated as self-proving.
  2. Federal executive execution: constitutional functions, appointments, agencies, delegations, and administrative sources are held behind source and evidence gates.
  3. Federal judiciary execution: courts, jurisdiction, cases, orders, judgments, remedies, and finality are separated from unsupported judicial effect.
  4. Federalism: state, territorial, tribal, local, and federal interfaces are modeled without inferring recognition, supremacy, preemption, immunity, or sovereignty.
  5. Election and public participation: qualification, administration, ballots, custody, canvass, certification, contest, and public participation are kept distinct and reviewable.
  6. National cross-branch checks and remedies: disputes, holds, corrections, stays, finality, release, and reopening are governed as explicit interfaces rather than assumed outcomes.

How to read this repository

Read from the system map outward, then use the records and conformance materials to test each claim. The repository is intentionally evidence-first: it records what was acquired, proposed, routed, held, tested, frozen, and released, without converting unresolved material into institutional fact.

What this release is not

It is not a live governmental register, an automated authority engine, legal advice, or a substitute for officials, courts, reviewers, or lawful process. Adoption of the reference framework does not transfer authority to any actor or system.
